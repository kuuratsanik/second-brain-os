# Retrieval practice at work

> Fictional. This page belongs to the demo vault; the people, organisations and articles in it are invented.

## What it is

A long-form article by Pieter Lund (an invented author) on keeping technical knowledge after a course or a reading list ends. It covers retrieval practice, which means testing yourself instead of rereading.

## Claims

- Rereading feels productive and produces little. Closing the material and writing down what you remember produces more.
- Spacing the recall attempts matters: the article suggests one day, three days and ten days after first contact.
- Short recall attempts of five to ten minutes are enough for working professionals; the article gives no data for that figure.
- Recall errors are useful. A wrong answer shows which page of the notes needs rewriting.
